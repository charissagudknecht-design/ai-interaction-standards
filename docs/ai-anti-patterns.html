AI Anti-Patterns

Common mistakes and harmful patterns to avoid when designing AI-powered features in Rosewood products.

Why Document Anti-Patterns?

Documenting what NOT to do is just as important as documenting best practices. Anti-patterns erode user trust, create poor experiences, and can lead to costly errors in a salon and spa management context. By explicitly cataloging these pitfalls, teams can proactively avoid them during design reviews and development, ensuring AI features enhance rather than undermine the user experience.

Overconfident AI

The Problem

Presenting AI output as definitive fact without confidence indicators misleads users into trusting suggestions that may be uncertain or incorrect. When an AI recommendation appears with the same visual weight as verified data, users cannot distinguish between high-confidence and low-confidence outputs.

[Insert bad example: AI suggestion shown without confidence score or source attribution]

The Fix

Always display a confidence level alongside AI-generated content, attribute the source of the suggestion, and provide a clear mechanism for users to override or dismiss the recommendation. Use the glow-ai-confidence component to visually communicate certainty levels.

Black Box Responses

The Problem

When AI provides an answer or recommendation with no explanation of how it arrived at that conclusion, users have no basis for evaluating the quality of the suggestion. This is especially problematic in business-critical contexts like scheduling optimization or revenue forecasting.

[Insert bad example: AI recommendation with no reasoning shown]

The Fix

Provide a "Why this suggestion?" expandable section that reveals the data points and reasoning behind the AI output. This transparency allows users to make informed decisions about whether to accept, modify, or reject the suggestion.

Unrecoverable Actions

The Problem

AI that automatically executes actions which cannot be undone poses significant risk. For example, automatically sending a client communication, canceling an appointment, or adjusting pricing without user confirmation can result in business harm and eroded trust.

[Insert bad example: AI auto-sends message without confirmation]

The Fix

Always require explicit user confirmation before executing destructive or external-facing actions. Present a preview of the action with a clear confirm/cancel dialog. For batch operations, show a summary of all changes before execution.

Notification Overload

The Problem

Surfacing too many AI-generated notifications, suggestions, and alerts drowns out important information and leads to notification fatigue. Users begin ignoring all notifications, including critical ones, when the volume becomes unmanageable.

[Insert bad example: screen cluttered with AI suggestion toasts]

The Fix

Batch AI notifications intelligently, prioritize based on urgency and relevance, and provide users with controls to mute or adjust the frequency of AI suggestions. Use a dedicated AI notification center rather than competing with system-level alerts.

Anthropomorphization

The Problem

Making AI seem human by using first-person language ("I think", "I believe"), human-like avatar faces, or emotional language creates false expectations about AI capabilities. Users may over-trust or develop inappropriate reliance on AI that presents itself as a human-like entity.

[Insert bad example: AI chatbot with human-like avatar saying "I feel this is the best option"]

The Fix

Use neutral, data-driven language such as "Based on historical data..." or "Suggested by AI analysis." Represent AI with abstract icons or the Rosewood AI brand mark rather than human-like avatars. Clearly label all AI-generated content with the AI attribution badge.

Checklist

Use this checklist during design reviews and development to verify that AI features avoid common anti-patterns.

Do

  • Show confidence levels
  • Provide explanations
  • Allow overrides
  • Batch notifications
  • Use neutral AI language
  • Log decisions

Don't

  • Hide AI reasoning
  • Auto-execute without confirmation
  • Overload with suggestions
  • Use human-like personas
  • Skip audit trails
  • Ignore error states
