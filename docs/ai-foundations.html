Foundations

The strategic guide for AI at Rosewood. From design principles to surface selection, this page tells the story of how Glow comes to life.

Core Design Principles

Six principles that govern every AI interaction in the Rosewood platform.

Transparency
Always indicate when content is AI-generated. Users must know when they're interacting with Glow.
Control
Users can always override, edit, or dismiss AI suggestions. The human has the final word.
Progressive Disclosure
Show AI complexity gradually, starting with simple outputs and offering depth on demand.
Contextual Relevance
AI features appear where they add value, not everywhere. Smart placement over saturation.
Graceful Degradation
When AI fails, the experience remains functional. Retry patterns, fallbacks, preserved context.
Consistent Identity
Glow has a recognizable visual presence. Ruby Berry accent, sparkle icon, and Glow attribution.

9 Composition Principles

Guardrails for how many AI elements can coexist on a single screen. Apply these when designing any surface that includes AI components.

PrincipleGuideline
1. Maximum AI DensityNo screen should surface more than 5 AI-related signals simultaneously. If adding a 6th, remove one or collapse two into progressive disclosure.
2. Progressive DensityEvery AI component must be designed in three densities: full panel (strategic), collapsed headline (workflow), and minimal badge (operational).
3. Transparency on DemandHeadline visible by default, detail one tap away, full reasoning two taps away. Never front-load full explanations.
4. Proactivity BudgetLimit AI-initiated interruptions by context: low on operational (1–2 per session), moderate on workflow (3–5), uncapped but prioritized on strategic.
5. The Subtraction TestFor every AI pattern you add, identify one legacy element to remove, simplify, or collapse. AI should reduce complexity, not increase it.
6. Error Invisibility GradientStrategic: full error + recovery options. Workflow: error + one fix. Operational: silent fallback to manual with no modal.
7. Feedback as ExhaustFeedback should be a byproduct of normal workflow (implicit correction logging), not a separate action. Explicit feedback only at natural pauses.
8. AI-Second, Not AI-FirstAI surfaces should live where work already happens — inline, contextual, embedded — not in separate dedicated AI screens.
9. Appropriate FrictionAdd deliberate pause points at irreversible moments: save, share, send, publish. Speed of creation must not bypass judgment at consequence.

Two Modes of AI Design

Determine the mode before choosing components. This is the single biggest upstream decision. AI-Enhanced screens are conventional interfaces where AI adds a layer. AI-Native screens are experiences built entirely around AI.

DimensionAI-EnhancedAI-Native
AI IdentityBadges, borders, attribution — AI must stand out from conventional UI.Minimal identity markers — the whole experience is AI, so labeling everything is noise.
When to Mark AIOn every AI-generated or AI-modified element.Only at judgment gates: before sending, publishing, confirming, paying.
AI Accent ColorUsed on all AI elements to distinguish them from the product’s primary palette.Reserved for “your judgment is needed here” moments — not for general AI presence.
WayfindingNudges, spotlights, and capability framing help users find AI features.Not needed — the user is already in an AI experience. Focus on task clarity instead.
Error HandlingAI errors are visually distinct from system errors (separate color, icon).Errors are just errors — no need to distinguish AI failures from other failures.
Feedback CaptureExplicit (thumbs up/down visible on AI outputs).Implicit by default (correction logging), explicit only at natural pause points.

The Governing Principle

Label AI where the user’s judgment is the last gate before a consequence. Everywhere else, let AI be infrastructure.

Composition Density

AI component density flexes by screen level, not by user role. Strategic screens get the richest AI treatment; operational screens stay out of the way.

Screen LevelExamplesAI DensityBehavior
StrategicReports, dashboards, analyticsHighFull reasoning, confidence indicators, source panels.
WorkflowAppointments, profiles, campaignsModerateCollapsed summaries, contextual nudges, detail on tap.
OperationalCheckout, booking, POSMinimalSilent autofill, near-invisible badges, quiet fallback to manual.

Every AI component should be designed in three densities so it can adapt to the screen level it appears on.

AI Maturity Model

Rosewood's AI features follow a progressive maturity model. Each level builds on the previous, expanding scope and autonomy.

Level 1
Assistive
AI provides suggestions that require explicit user approval before any action.
Level 2
Collaborative
AI and user work together. Glow drafts, proposes, and handles routine tasks with oversight.
Level 3
Autonomous
AI executes approved tasks independently within guardrails, reporting outcomes to the user.

Surface Specs

Four distinct surfaces define how Glow appears across the Rosewood platform. Each has a specific trigger, transition, and use case.

Assistant Overlay
Header Glow mark
Fade-in center modal + backdrop blur

Use When

  • Global tasks: "Find my 10 AM appointment"
  • Quick queries that don't need page context
  • Command palette-style interactions

Don't Use When

  • Task requires seeing dashboard data simultaneously
  • Multi-step workflows with form inputs
Copilot Sidebar
Floating FAB
Slide-in from right, 16px floating gutter

Use When

  • Working alongside a specific workflow (campaigns, scheduling)
  • AI needs page context to give relevant suggestions
  • Persistent conversation during multi-step tasks

Don't Use When

  • Quick one-off questions (use Overlay instead)
  • Content creation that needs a wide canvas
Full-Page Canvas
Inline "Optimize" Button
Full-page takeover → split-view (thread + artifacts)

Use When

  • High-density creation: strategies, reports, content briefs
  • AI produces multiple artifacts that need review
  • Analytics and business intelligence queries

Don't Use When

  • Simple yes/no decisions
  • Tasks that require real-time dashboard visibility
Inline Widget
Contextual Sparkle
Inline expansion or popover (no page shift)

Use When

  • Contextual AI within a form field or card
  • Quick suggestions: "Optimize this subject line"
  • AI-enhanced inputs that don't need a full conversation

Don't Use When

  • Complex multi-turn conversations
  • Tasks that generate multiple deliverables
